This chapter introduces the core idea of uncertainty and explains why it should be treated as a central concept in modeling, research, and decision-making.

## Why uncertainty matters

Uncertainty is not a weakness of a model. It is often a property of the world, the data, and the decision process itself.

## A basic view

We often distinguish between different kinds of uncertainty:

- uncertainty from randomness in the data,
- uncertainty from model misspecification,
- uncertainty from incomplete information,
- uncertainty from the limits of measurement.

## Types of uncertainty

A common distinction is between aleatoric uncertainty, which arises from inherent randomness in the data, and epistemic uncertainty, which reflects what the model does not know because the information is limited or missing.

This makes uncertainty a foundational concept across statistics, machine learning, and scientific inquiry.
